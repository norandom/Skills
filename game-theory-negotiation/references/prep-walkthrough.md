# Preparation walkthrough for a new negotiator

Use this when the user is new to negotiation or arrives with an unstructured situation. Lead them step by step into a prepared position. Do not dump the whole framework at once. Ask for the next missing input, fill what can be inferred, and keep a running brief.

## Output: the ready-room brief

End with a compact brief the negotiator can use before the call:

- Situation: one-line deal description.
- Goal: what they want and why.
- Numbers: BATNA, reservation price, ZOPA estimate, surplus estimate, aspiration ladder.
- Truth checks: claims that need evidence before they get priced into the deal.
- Scenarios: future conditions that change payoffs or leverage.
- Offer plan: opening position, target, concession schedule, closing sweetener.
- Trade list: non-price terms and their values.
- Guardrails: what to refuse, when to pause, when to escalate to higher authority.
- Diagram: the draw.io view to keep open during the conversation.
- Next words: the first two or three sentences to say in the negotiation.

## Walkthrough

### 1. Frame the deal

Ask:
- Who is negotiating with whom?
- What is being traded?
- Is this one issue, or are there several terms?
- Is this a one-off deal or an ongoing relationship?
- Who can approve the final agreement?

If the user is vague, produce a first draft and mark unknowns instead of stalling.

### 2. Separate facts, claims, and guesses

Ask what each party says is true. Mark each item as fact, claim, or guess.

Route to `investigation-theory` when a claim changes price, timing, leverage, trust, authority, or legal exposure. Examples: "costs went up," "we have another buyer," "this is our final offer," "the deadline is fixed," "procurement requires this."

Return with:
- Claim.
- Source.
- Evidence available.
- Evidence missing.
- Who benefits if the claim is false.
- Negotiation impact.

### 3. Check the future before pricing the present

Ask what could change before the agreement is signed or performed.

Route to `strategic-foresight-viz` when external conditions change BATNA, demand, supply, cost, timing, regulation, competition, or risk. Keep the result small: best case, likely case, worst case, and early warning signs.

Return with:
- Scenario name.
- What changes.
- Which payoff changes.
- What signal would confirm it.

### 4. Test assumptions that drive the move

Route to `intana-viz` when the preparation depends on a disputed assumption or a theory of the other side's behavior. Use:

- ACH when several explanations could fit the facts.
- Red Teaming when you need the other side's likely moves.
- Key Assumptions Check when one belief drives the strategy.
- Pre-Mortem when the deal can fail after apparent agreement.

Return with the assumption, confidence, failure mode, and what would change the plan.

### 5. Set the numbers

Fill in:

- BATNA: best outside option if no deal happens.
- Reservation price: the floor, not the target.
- ZOPA estimate: overlap between both sides' likely reservation prices.
- Surplus estimate: value available to split.
- Aspiration ladder: BPO, Ambitious Target, Target, Reservation Level, No-Deal Outcome.

If the BATNA is uncertain, use three-point or four-point estimates from `references/frameworks.md`.

### 6. Build the trade list

Use `assets/virtual-price-list-template.md`.

Capture:
- Price.
- Delivery or timing.
- Payment terms.
- Warranty or support.
- Exclusivity.
- Volume or commitment.
- Implementation help.
- Low-cost sweetener held for the close.

Mark each term as high value to me, high value to them, cheap for me, or expensive for me.

### 7. Choose the diagram

Use `references/drawio-concepts.md`.

- New negotiator, many parties: player and incentive map.
- Truthfulness is uncertain: information asymmetry map.
- Timing and responses matter: sequential move tree.
- Two clear choices per side: payoff matrix.
- Many tradable terms: deal architecture map.

Generate one diagram first. Add a second only if it answers a different question.

### 8. Plan the conversation

Write the opening in plain speech:

- Acknowledge the shared goal.
- State the opening offer or counter-offer.
- Attach reasons to terms, not emotions.
- Keep the floor private.
- Prepare a reversible no for pressure.

Example structure:

"I want to get this done, and I think we can. Based on the scope and timing, I can do X if we include Y and Z. If that does not work on your side, let's trade terms rather than just move the price."

### 9. Rehearse pressure moments

Prepare exact responses for:

- They demand one more concession.
- They claim a deadline.
- They say they have another offer.
- They ask for your best and final.
- They appeal to fairness without naming a criterion.
- They move a non-price term into the deal at the end.

### 10. Stop condition

Stop preparation when the user has:

- A private floor.
- An ambitious target.
- A credible opening.
- A concession schedule.
- A trade list.
- A truth-check list.
- A diagram.
- A first script.

If any of these are missing, say exactly what remains and ask only for that piece.
