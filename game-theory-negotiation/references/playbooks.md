# Playbooks: worked sequences

Four compact walkthroughs that chain the concepts (`references/core-concepts.md`), tactics (`references/tactics.md`), and frameworks (`references/frameworks.md`) into runnable sequences. Adapt the numbers; keep the order.

---

## Playbook 1: Buy/sell a single asset (distributive, one issue)

*Scenario: buying a used car listed at EUR18,000; you'd pay at most EUR16,000; you believe the seller paid about EUR13,500 and needs to sell.*

1. **Set the numbers.** Your reservation price (floor) = EUR16,000. Estimate seller's RP at about EUR13,500, so ZOPA is about 13.5k-16k and surplus is about EUR2,500. Improve your BATNA first: line up a second comparable car so you can walk.
2. **Build the aspiration ladder.** BPO about EUR13,500 (their floor); Ambitious Target about EUR14,500; Target about EUR15,200; Reservation Level = EUR16,000; No-Deal = keep searching. Focus on EUR14,500, not EUR16,000.
3. **Plan degressive bids.** Target price = middle of the range, about EUR14,750. Open with a plausible maximum position: EUR13,800, low but defensible because comparable cars are going for that. Concessions halve the gap to your target: 13,800, 14,275, 14,510, 14,630, with precise numbers.
4. **Hold the line.** Each seller demand gets a counter-offer, never a flat no. If pushed toward EUR16k, use a reversible no: "Not at that price, but I can do EUR14,630 today."
5. **Close with a sweetener, not more price.** When steps shrink to about EUR50, stop on price and add an in-kind token from the virtual price list: "I'll collect it myself and pay cash today."
6. **Defend the close.** Between handshake and paperwork, watch for a nibble: "leave the floor mats" or "I want a full tank." Price-tag and isolate it, or counter-nibble.
7. **Verify.** Did you beat your BATNA (the second car)? Settled near the AT? Win-win confirmed.

## Playbook 2: Procurement with multiple suppliers (negotiauction)

*Scenario: sourcing a component from 4 credible suppliers.*

1. **Choose the mechanism, not just tactics.** With multiple credible bidders, run a **negotiauction** (`references/frameworks.md` §7) rather than four separate bilateral talks.
2. **Write the auction contract.** Publish a strict, transparent, pre-announced rule set: bidding rounds, evaluation formula (price + non-price), information policy. Bind yourself to it.
3. **Make the commitment credible.** State and mean that you will not leak one supplier's price to another. Your reputation enforces the rules; a leak would be costly, so suppliers trust the process and submit their best price.
4. **Use a virtual price list** to score non-price terms (lead time, quality, warranty) on a common money scale, so bids are comparable.
5. **Block holdup up front.** Tie payment to milestones, add automated triggers such as penalty or escrow clauses, and consider reciprocal commitments for relationship-specific tooling (`references/frameworks.md` section 4).
6. **Consider incentive compatibility.** If you want truthful bids, lean toward a Vickrey-style rule so suppliers bid their real value rather than gaming the gap.
7. **Verify fairness and win-win.** All bidders faced identical rules; the winner beats your BATNA; terms were enlarged via trades, not just crushed on price.

## Playbook 3: Design an incentive / profit-share

*Scenario: setting variable pay for a deal-maker or partner.*

1. **Confirm a variable component is warranted.** If effort materially drives the outcome, a profit share can lift performance above a fixed salary (`references/frameworks.md` section 8).
2. **Interrogate the size.** Do not default to a round 50%. Anchor on benchmarks: investment banking may be about 2% of transaction volume, roughly 15% of value created. Size the share to the agent's actual marginal contribution.
3. **Pick a fairness criterion.** Tie the share to productivity / value created (`references/frameworks.md` section 5) rather than inputs, and make the metric explicit and measurable.
4. **Guard against gaming and holdup.** Use milestones and clawbacks so the agent cannot capture rents on luck or hold the principal up after a specific investment.
5. **Model uncertainty.** The payout depends on uncertain outcomes. Apply a three-point estimate and discount by the relevant risk aversion to value the package realistically for both sides.

## Playbook 4: Defend against holdup / last-minute renegotiation

*Scenario: counterpart reopens terms after you've sunk a relationship-specific investment.*

1. **Diagnose: nibble or holdup?** Small grab between handshake and signature = nibble (`references/tactics.md` section 5). Structural grab after a sunk specific investment = holdup (`references/frameworks.md` section 4).
2. **For a nibble:** price-tag and isolate, counter-nibble, then threaten to reopen the entire negotiation.
3. **For holdup:** invoke the pre-agreed protections if you built them: milestones, automated penalty/escrow triggers, reciprocal commitments. If you did not, fall back on BATNA (can you switch?) and higher authority ("the board won't approve a change") to buy time and a face-saving exit.
4. **Re-anchor with a counter-offer**, never a flat capitulation; keep the rest of the deal frozen while the disputed point is handled separately.
5. **Post-mortem:** if you were exposed, it's because protections weren't designed in at structuring time. Add them to the template for next time.
