# EBITDA

EBITDA = Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization. It is a **non-GAAP** measure of pure operating profit, stripped of financing policy (interest), tax policy (taxes), and asset-life accounting (D&A). Use it to compare operating performance across companies with different capital structures, tax situations, and asset bases.

## Two ways to calculate

- **Bottom-up:** Net Income + Interest + Taxes + Depreciation + Amortization.
- **Top-down:** Revenue − COGS − Operating expenses (before D&A). Equivalent to EBIT + D&A.

## Bojan's four EBITDA KPIs

| KPI | Formula | Reads |
|---|---|---|
| Absolute EBITDA | bottom-up or top-down | scale of operating profit |
| EBITDA Margin | EBITDA / Revenues | operating profitability % |
| EBITDA-to-Interest Coverage | EBITDA / Interest expense | debt-servicing headroom (lending) |
| EBITDA per Employee | EBITDA / Employee number | workforce productivity |

## Applications

- **Company valuation** — EV/EBITDA multiples; also the starting point for indirect operating cash flow (adjust for NWC change and CAPEX).
- **Industry benchmarking** — the "great equalizer": neutralizes capital structure and tax, so peers compare cleanly.
- **Operational performance** — margin trend and EBITDA/employee track operating efficiency.
- **Lending assessment** — EBITDA-interest coverage tells a lender how safely debt is serviced.

## Considerations before trusting an EBITDA number

Consistency of definition, industry standards, company life cycle (startups run low or negative), economic conditions, and **always treat cash flow separately**. Evaluate EBITDA against the debt load, never alone.

## Improvement levers

Capitalize qualifying expenditures, pricing strategy, cost control and supplier-term renegotiation, process automation, inventory optimization, revenue diversification, and talent retention.

## Adjusted EBITDA (M&A / due diligence)

Normalizations: add back excess owner compensation, stock-based comp, one-off GAAP items, and restate related-party transfer pricing to arm's length. **Caveat:** management-adjusted EBITDA can flatter results — assess each add-back critically.

## Controversies — why EBITDA is dangerous alone

- Non-GAAP and inconsistently defined across companies.
- Manipulation risk — can mask a dangerously high debt load.
- Excludes real costs (interest, taxes, D&A are genuine cash or economic costs).
- **It is NOT cash flow** — "you can't take EBITDA home." It ignores CAPEX and working-capital swings.
- Meaningless in isolation — company value depends on 40+ factors (see `valuation-dcf-wacc.md`).
