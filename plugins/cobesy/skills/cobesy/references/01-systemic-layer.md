# Layer 1 — Systemic Base

**Inputs:** `cultural_assumptions` · **Output:** `framing_protocol`
**Sources:** Schein *Organizational Culture and Leadership*; Edmondson *The
Fearless Organization*; Scientist Mode (Grant, folded in).

This layer maps and neutralizes the deep, unwritten rules of the enterprise. It
treats the organization as a **survival system**, not a political chart. Get this
wrong and every downstream artifact is rejected by the group's defense
mechanisms — regardless of clarity or routing.

---

## Schein — three levels of culture

| Level | What it is | Visibility | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Artifacts** | Visible structures, rituals, tools, office layout, how meetings run | Easy to see, **hard to decipher** | You observe behavior but misread the *why*. |
| **Espoused values** | Strategies, goals, stated philosophies ("we value candor") | What the group *says* | Often contradicts the artifacts. |
| **Basic underlying assumptions** | Unconscious, taken-for-granted beliefs; the real driver of behavior | Invisible, **anxiety-reducing** | This is what actually governs. Never attack it head-on. |

### Diagnose the hidden assumption
The basic underlying assumption is **the belief that resolves the contradiction
between espoused values and observed artifacts.**

1. **Observe artifacts.** What do people actually do, reward, avoid?
2. **Collect espoused values.** What do they say they believe?
3. **Detect discrepancies.** Where does talk diverge from action? These friction
   points are the doorways.
4. **Name the assumption.** State the belief that, if true, would make the
   contradiction rational. Example: espoused "we take smart risks" + artifact
   "no one who led a failed project got promoted" → assumption: *"past failures
   dictate your career ceiling."*

Culture is a **group defense mechanism that reduces anxiety.** You do not argue
it away; you design *around* it and create conditions where a new assumption can
safely form.

### Diagnostic question bank (replaces icebreakers)
Use **diagnostic-contrast prompts**, not warm-ups:
- "Tell me about a project here that failed. What really happened to the people on it?"
- "What is something everyone here knows but no one writes down?"
- "What would a new hire do that would quietly end their credibility?"
- "Where do our stated values and our actual rewards disagree?"

## Edmondson — psychological safety × accountability

Safety alone produces comfort, not performance. Pair it with high standards.

```
 high │  ANXIETY zone        │   LEARNING zone        │
 acct │  (fear, silence,     │   (high performance,   │
      │   toxic)             │    candor + standards) │  ← TARGET
      │──────────────────────┼────────────────────────│
  low │  APATHY zone         │   COMFORT zone         │
 acct │  (checked out)       │   (nice, not driven)   │
      └──────────────────────┴────────────────────────┘
         low safety              high safety
```

**Gate:** if the org sits in the **Anxiety** quadrant (low safety, high
accountability), raise safety *before* pushing content. Content delivered into
fear is not heard.

### Leader toolkit (the three moves)
1. **Frame the work** as a learning problem under uncertainty (not a delivery of
   the already-known). Emphasize interdependence and what is genuinely unknown.
2. **Invite participation** through genuine inquiry and situational humility. Ask
   real questions; create structures (forums, norms) that make speaking up easy.
3. **Respond productively** to voice: appreciate the contribution, destigmatize
   intelligent failure, and *sanction* clearly blameworthy acts so standards stay real.

### Three types of failure
| Type | Blameworthy? | Response |
|---|---|---|
| **Preventable** (known process, deviation) | Yes | Correct; address cause. |
| **Complex** (system interaction, novel mix) | No | Analyze; build resilience. |
| **Intelligent** (thoughtful experiment at the frontier) | No — **celebrate** | Reward; harvest the learning. |

## Scientist Mode (falsifiability)

Treat every proposal as a **hypothesis**, not a position to defend. Adam Grant's
modes: think like a **scientist**, not a preacher, prosecutor, or politician.

- **Ban** the Prosecutor (out to win) and Politician (out to please) personas in
  strategy and architecture discussions.
- Every proposal must carry an explicit **disproof condition**: *"We are wrong if
  ___."* If a team cannot state what evidence would prove them wrong, the agenda
  item is **rejected** until they can.
- This converts opinion-defense into shared truth-seeking and lowers status risk
  (you are testing an idea, not yourself).

## Output: `framing_protocol`

Produce a short protocol the user can act on:
- **Named basic assumption(s)** you are designing around.
- **Org quadrant** + whether a safety-raising step is required first.
- **Framing sentences**: how to frame the work as a learning problem.
- **What not to say** (phrases that would trip the defense mechanism).
- **Disproof condition** attached to the core proposal.

Then proceed to the cognitive layer to compress the actual content.
