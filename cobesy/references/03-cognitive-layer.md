# Layer 3 — Cognitive Interface

**Inputs:** `raw_knowledge` · **Output:** `agenda_structure`
**Sources:** Minto *The Pyramid Principle*; Dirksen *Design for How People
Learn*; Knowles *The Adult Learner* (andragogy).

This layer reduces information footprint while preserving conceptual nuance. It
designs content around the cognitive boundaries of the human brain and the
specific psychology of adult learners.

---

## Minto — the Pyramid Principle

Lead with the **conclusion**. Group support so that every idea is summarized by
the one above it.

### The three rules
1. Ideas at any level must **summarize** the ideas grouped below them.
2. Ideas in each grouping must be **the same kind** of idea.
3. Ideas in each grouping must be **logically ordered**.

### MECE
Each grouping must be **Mutually Exclusive** (no overlap) and **Collectively
Exhaustive** (nothing missing). This is the structural integrity check — and the
first item in the validation gate.

### SCQA — the introduction
Open every brief or agenda with:
- **Situation** — the stable, agreed context.
- **Complication** — what changed / the tension.
- **Question** — the question the complication raises.
- **Answer** — your conclusion (which becomes the top of the pyramid).

### Four logical orderings within a group
Deductive · Chronological · Structural · Comparative (by degree of importance).

### Build process
- **Top-down (preferred):** box the subject → state the question → give the
  answer → set the situation → add the complication → draw the key line →
  attach support.
- **Bottom-up (fallback):** list all points → find relationships → infer the
  conclusion → lift it to the top.

The payoff: the audience grasps the entire logical architecture in the first ~60
seconds. No long historical preamble.

## Dirksen — cognitive load and the learner

### Five gap types (diagnose what's actually missing)
| Gap | Real need |
|---|---|
| **Knowledge** | Information / access (often a job aid, not a course) |
| **Skill** | Deliberate **practice** over time |
| **Motivation** | Belief + often **unlearning** an old habit |
| **Environment** | Tools, incentives, removing obstacles |
| **Communication** | Clear directions (the cheapest fix, often skipped) |

Do not treat a motivation or environment gap as a knowledge gap. Adding content
to a non-knowledge gap increases load and fails.

### Working memory is the gatekeeper
Capacity ≈ **7±2** chunks; it is short and fragile. Overload blocks transfer to
long-term memory. Everything in the validation gate's "Load" check comes from here.

### Elephant and Rider
The **Rider** is rational (small, tires fast); the **Elephant** is emotional
(huge, decides). To move behavior you must talk to the **Elephant**: stories,
urgency, surprise, social proof, concrete stakes, and pre-built habits — not just
logical argument to the Rider.

### Behavior change is a process, not an event
- **TAM**: people adopt what is both **useful** and **easy to use**.
- Pair **modeling** with **practice**; apply to the learner's *real* problem.
- **Reinforce over time**; one exposure is not learning.

### Load-reduction toolkit
- **Chunking** — group into 7±2 meaningful units.
- **Scaffolding** — support early, remove as competence grows.
- **Knowledge in the world** — job aids, decision trees, references (don't make
  them memorize what they can look up).
- **Supply caching** — put the resource where the work happens.
- **Pacing** — alternate uphill effort with coasting to reach the flow channel.
- **Implementation intentions** — "if X, then I will Y" beats vague resolve.

## Knowles — andragogy (for adults, seniors, experts)

Adults are not big children. Six assumptions:
1. **Need to know** — tell them *why* before *what*.
2. **Self-concept** — they are self-directing and **resent being treated as
   children**.
3. **Experience** — their experience is a rich resource; **devaluing it is
   experienced as rejecting them**.
4. **Readiness** — tied to real-life developmental tasks and problems.
5. **Orientation** — **problem/task/life-centered**, not subject-centered.
6. **Motivation** — internal (mastery, relevance) > external.

### Process design
Establish a climate of mutuality, informality, and adult safety → plan
**mutually** (commitment is proportional to participation) → let them
**self-diagnose** needs → set objectives together → design experiential,
problem-centered activities → use learning contracts → evaluate as **mutual
re-diagnosis**.

> Seniors and experts share the **same working-memory limits** as juniors. The
> difference is they have more experience to honor and stronger reactance to
> being lectured. Lead with their problem, not your curriculum.

## Output: `agenda_structure`

Produce a top-down hierarchy:
- **SCQA opener** (Situation / Complication / Question / Answer).
- **The governing conclusion** as the single top box.
- **MECE key line** (3–5 grouped supporting points), each chunked to load.
- **Where the Elephant is addressed** (the story/stakes, not just the data).
- **Job aids / references** moved out of working memory into the world.
- For senior audiences: the **problem framing** that earns their attention first.
