# Cash flow and working capital

Profit is an opinion; cash is a fact. EBITDA and net income ignore changes in working capital, CAPEX, taxes, and interest timing — so a profitable company can still run out of cash. This reference covers the cash flow statement (both methods), the 13-week forecast, and the working-capital levers that free or trap cash.

Notes layer: personal notes about `Cash Flow Handbook.pdf`, `3 Statements model.xlsx`, and `Budgeting model.xlsx` from Bojan Radojicic / bojanfin.com materials. This is not original source text. Pair with `assets/thirteen-week-cashflow.md`.

## Cash vs profit

"You can't take EBITDA home." EBITDA omits WC changes, CAPEX, taxes, and interest. Cash can be tied up in inventory and receivables even while the P&L shows profit. Always treat cash flow as a **separate** analysis from earnings.

Definitions: **Cash** = cash on hand + demand deposits. **Cash equivalents** = highly liquid, maturity ≤ 3 months. Bank overdrafts → financing. Dividends / interest / taxes paid → operating.

## Direct vs indirect

| Method | What it shows | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct** | Lists operating receipts and payments | Short-term (weekly / 13-week) |
| **Indirect** | Net income + non-cash +/- WC changes | Monthly / annual statements |

Three sections in both: **operating, investing, financing.**

Indirect build (links the statements):
```
Net income -> add back non-cash (D&A) -> +/- change in CA/CL (working capital)  = operating
                                       -> investing (CAPEX, other)
                                       -> financing (debt, dividends)
Cash beginning + Total CF = Cash ending   (= Balance Sheet Cash)
```

## Working capital

Two formulas, two purposes:

1. **Liquidity view:** NWC = Current Assets − Current Liabilities.
2. **Cash-planning view:** NWC = CA (excl. cash) − CL (excl. financial debt).

Components and projection drivers:

| Component | Projection |
|---|---|
| Accounts Receivable | AR = DSO × Revenue / 365 |
| Inventory | Inv = DIO × COGS / 365 |
| Accounts Payable | AP = DPO × COGS / 365 |

Direction of cash: a rise in CA (AR, Inventory) **ties up** cash (negative CF); a rise in AP **releases** cash (positive CF).

**Cash Conversion Cycle:** CCC = DIO + DSO − DPO. Shorter = more cash-rich. Worked sensitivities: DPO 60→75 = +$863K; DSO →59 = +$1,200K; DPO →30 = −$1,455K.

## The 13-week cash flow forecast

13 weekly columns; **column 1 = actual** (don't overwrite). Direct method.

- **Inflows:** AR collection + new sales + other.
- **Outflows:** supplier payments (by aging) + payroll + loan schedules.
- **Output:** weekly ending cash — an early-warning radar for liquidity gaps.

## Cash levers (the consultation layer)

**Eight ways to increase cash flow:** (1) faster collection / reduce DSO ~5 days; (2) sales efficiency, upsell, pricing; (3) supplier discounts / purchase pricing; (4) JIT stock / production efficiency; (5) efficient CAPEX / sell idle assets; (6) loan restructuring; (7) financing arrangements; (8) post-merger synergies.

| Lever | Moves |
|---|---|
| **DSO** | Automate dunning, advance payments (~40%), interest on overdue, credit limits — "the due date is money in the bank" |
| **DPO** | Pay later without harming the relationship, avoid fixed pay-dates, take early-pay discounts only if margin justifies |
| **DIO** | Align purchasing to demand, JIT, cut obsolete stock, review safety stock |

Assign explicit **ownership** of each working-capital line — unowned WC drifts.
